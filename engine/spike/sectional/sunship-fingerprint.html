Where the turning actually happens

Each wedge is one of 48 ribbons, drawn at its own azimuth. Length and colour both encode F2a, the absolute turning accumulated late in the earned domain (zones 4–5). θ = 0° is up, so θ = 180° is the flat underside.

The disagreement is up–down, and it sits forward

Turning accumulated in zone 1 — the first earned zone, just aft of the nose — plotted right around the body. The two lobes are the lower quarters; the troughs are up and the sides.

Reading this

The underside is quiet

θ 172.5–195° returns F2a of 0.09–0.12°. A flat surface has no turning to accumulate, and the operator reports that faithfully.

The upper quarters do the work

θ 37.5–52.5° and its mirror carry 36.2–37.0° — the whole aft burden is concentrated in four diagonal sectors.

Forward, not aft

The 22.4° directional disagreement lives in zone 1. By zones 4–5 the opposing sides have largely converged.

The pipeline checks out

Port and starboard agree to 0.00° on a real production mesh, through hull, skin and ribbons, with nothing tuned to make it happen.

What this is, and is not. This is a geometric fingerprint. It says the bare Sunship lies far outside the present measured analogue corpus in absolute ribbon turning, distributes that turning unusually evenly in the median, and carries strong directional disagreement that the scalar representation cannot express. It does not say any of that is aerodynamically bad: the reference corpus is axisymmetric, so a large F2a against it is a statement about geometry, not about adverse pressure gradient. No measured non-axisymmetric analogue corpus exists to support a drag claim. Nothing here alters Cd, calibration, or the production estimator, which stays byte-for-byte unchanged.
